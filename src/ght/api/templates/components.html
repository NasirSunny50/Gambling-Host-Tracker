Absence — two meanings

Not applicable {{ ui.na() }} a name-only payee has no number and no bank — there is nothing there to collect
Unknown {{ ui.unknown() }} a numbered account whose holder or bank was not found — we failed to collect it

Design notes

Name-only vs numbered payee. Both live in one table because the reader’s question — who receives the deposits — is the same. Their empty cells render as an em-dash marked not applicable, never as blank. That is deliberately distinct from an account with an unknown holder, which reads as an italic unknown. A blank cell would let the reader mistake “there is none to collect” for “we failed to collect it”. Both kinds open a page showing the screenshot of where they were published — for a name-only payee that picture is the whole of the evidence, since there is no number to carry anywhere else.

Waiting for sign-in. The hardest moment gets its own treatment: a calm blue rather than red, a “not an error” eyebrow, a countdown, and three unmissable facts — it is waiting for you, a browser window opened elsewhere, and it resumes on its own. Distinct from progress at a glance, and distinct from failure by colour and copy.

Exact timestamps. Relative ages (“3h ago”) read nicely and hide the thing a case file has to state. Every time shown in a list or on a detail page is the exact local timestamp.

No alerts screen. Health lives in the data itself: the note on every run says what it could not read and why, so nobody has to visit a log to know fetching is working.
